---
name: Whole-fish inventory
description: Product, pricing, stock, POS, and restoration rules for whole-fish and bulk-portion modes.
---

Each fish species is a single product, not a set of weight sub-products. When no explicit category is assigned, use the species/product name as its category in inventory and POS views. Fish products have two stock modes:

1. **Individual fish:** record each incoming fish as its own batch with its weight in kilograms. The product's customer selling rate per kilogram determines each fish's total price (`weight × rate`), rounded to the nearest whole rupee; POS shows each eligible fish separately and carries its exact batch ID through sale, deduction, and restoration.
2. **Bulk portions:** batches contain shared kilogram stock. Each sale option can use an exact weight or an optional minimum–maximum range. Exact options keep the configured weight; ranged options use the midpoint for displayed price, estimated pack availability, and stock deduction. Calculated portion prices are rounded to the nearest whole rupee. POS and customer-menu data expose the range and average-based price. Order items must send the pack count as `quantity` and the effective weight as `bulkWeightKg`. Admin POS can adjust grams for a specific order line; store the adjusted weight and proportional rounded price without changing the catalog option. Only authorized admin POS orders may use unconfigured weights; customer orders remain limited to configured weights. A sale deducts `pack count × effective weight` from eligible kilogram batches, and cancellation restores the exact allocations.

Only mode changes with no remaining stock are allowed. Store the fish rate and mode separately from the product's standard price and unit so switching modes does not overwrite the original catalog configuration. Do not silently rewrite existing individual-fish prices when loading a legacy product with varying historical prices; recalculate them only after the selling rate is explicitly changed. Calculate shelf life as the calendar-day difference between received date and expiry date, ignoring times. Derive it when older batches have both dates but no saved shelf-life value.

**Why:** The user distinguishes fish sold individually from products such as prawns purchased in bulk and sold in configured portions, with the same species remaining one catalog product. POS must account for actual grams in an order without changing catalog options; custom weights must remain order-specific and authorized. Calculated individual-fish and bulk-portion prices should be whole rupees; ranged portions use the midpoint for price and stock calculations, while exact options retain the configured weight. The inventory/order boundary validates selected weights, so fixtures and integrations must preserve the same pack-count/weight fields as POS. Legacy whole-fish batches can have different historical price-per-kg values, so deriving a default rate must not alter their saved prices; keeping the standard catalog price and unit also makes a mode change reversible.

**How to apply:** Use the individual-fish mode for one-fish-per-batch stock and the bulk-portion mode for shared kilogram batches. Keep exact weights or ranges on the root fish product; for a range, derive the midpoint from its bounds rather than trusting a client-supplied average. Round individual-fish and portion prices to whole rupees, and calculate stock deductions from the effective weight. Send pack count in `quantity` and the effective weight in `bulkWeightKg`; POS gram changes affect only the order line, update its price proportionally, and must not rewrite catalog options. Mark custom weights server-side only for authorized admin POS requests, and continue rejecting unconfigured weights from customer orders. Persist exact batch allocations on sales so restores return stock to its source batches. Change modes only after stock is zero. Preserve date-only shelf-life arithmetic so times and timezone offsets do not change day counts.
